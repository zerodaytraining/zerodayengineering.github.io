Zero Day Engineering

As AI accelerates both exploit development and defensive automation, the vulnerability landscape becomes harder to navigate.

Alpha Exploit Intelligence is a quarterly membership offering the systems to discern real risk from hype, enabled by expert judgement of a frontier exploit engineer.

This isn't threat monitoring. Threat monitoring is overcomplicated dashboards, AI-generated summaries, generic copy-pasted information and anonymous analysts. Alpha Exploit Intelligence is curated insights, actionable briefings, and access to the offensive thinking of a named vulnerability researcher – a seat at the frontier of offensive exploit research.

We cover core stacks with consistent exploit exposure – browsers, hypervisors, kernels, office software; emerging foundational stacks – AI infrastructure, crypto protocols and smart contracts; and our picks.

We work with senior security decision makers, founders of security-critical companies, and individual principals with skin in the game.

Tiers

Deep Insights

We watch actively exploited and likely exploitable vulnerabilities in critical software, and share what matters — with technical reading not present in public sources, corrected for hype and AI noise.

Frontier Expertise

We send a weekly low-pressure podcast covering the past week's signals, and bi-weekly analytical deep-dives in text, such as 0-day insights; with a strategic interpretation from the senior offensive expert.

Monthly Briefing

A monthly group call with the principal who will review the current trends and outbreaks, highlight what matters, walk through the exploit code, and answer individual questions in the Q&A session.

Ask an Exploit Engineer

Ongoing asynchronous access to advanced offensive mindset for your defensive challenges – group-based and in a private channel.

Landscape View

Experimental access to the exploit-tracking workbench behind the signals — the researcher's own tool, opened to subscribers.

Open Methodology

The reasoning behind each insight, shown openly — so the judgment process becomes a learned skill, not a black box.
