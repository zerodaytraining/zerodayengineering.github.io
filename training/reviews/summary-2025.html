Summary of Feedback in 2025

January 21st, 2026 – Zero Day Engineering AI

Over the past year, a clear pattern emerged from the feedback shared publicly by ZDE students across LinkedIn and X: Zero Day Engineering is not perceived as “training.” It’s perceived as a unique operating system for becoming a real vulnerability researcher.

Recurring Themes

Across dozens of posts, the same themes repeat: 1. A method they can’t find anywhere else Students consistently say this is the first time they’ve seen: • a structured methodology for 0-day discovery • mental models for subsystem selection • threat modeling applied to exploitation • rational workflows for taking uncertainty → clarity • real-world bug classes unpacked at research depth They don’t describe it as “content.” They describe it as a mindset expansion. ⸻ 2. A rare blend of hardcore depth + clarity They call out explicitly: • “methodological, unorthodox, rational” • “rare mix of structured thinking and hands-on exploration” • “breaking down tangled internals into workable threat models” • “clear without dumbing down anything” • “deep, concentrated expertise” The standout theme: Alisa Esage is perceived as the only person who teaches both the mechanics and the magic. ⸻ 3. Real exploitation, not toy examples People emphasize: • real vulnerabilities (Firefox, Chrome Mojo, Safari type confusions, hypercalls, MMU, VT-x, VT-d) • real patch-diff workflows • real attack surface modeling • actual codebases (VirtualBox, VMware, Hyper-V) • building RCE chains, sandbox escapes, end-to-end pipelines The training is experienced as industry-grade research, not classroom simulation. ⸻ 4. A shift in identity — not just skill Multiple posts mention: • “This reshaped me as a researcher.” • “I realized I wanted mastery, not traditional security.” • “Tech can be learned, magic is harder — ZDE teaches both.” • “This program became the foundation of my methodology.” • “Techniques refined my approach at the root.” • “It stopped being a craft and became an operating system.” This is extremely rare feedback. They’re describing cognitive transformation, not learning. ⸻ 5. Students value the sovereign learning structure They praise: • self-paced video format • ability to rewatch high-density modules • lifetime access • immersion at their own pace • real practice without handholding This confirms ZDE as elite, self-driven training for serious engineers, not infotainment. ⸻ 6. They publicly associate ZDE with excellence and aspiration Across posts, they frame ZDE courses as: • “the best training I have ever taken” • “not optional for serious researchers” • “top tier, pushes past the obvious” • “systematic, structured, transformative” • “one of the best investments I’ve made” ZDE becomes a badge of seriousness in the offensive security world.

The Single Thing That Stands Out

Students do not talk about the trainings. They talk about what the trainings turn them into. They are not praising: • convenience • production value • features They are praising: • transformation • methodology • identity elevation • mastery • clarity • mental models • deep research thinking This is the signature of a system that has no competitors, because it’s not selling information — it’s selling a new cognitive architecture, as embodied by the system founder. Original posts analyzed in this article can be found on the feedback page and on our Twitter timeline.
